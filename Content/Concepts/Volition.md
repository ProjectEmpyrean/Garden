>   - Cessation of feeling and perception

> ## Optimizing Volition
> 
> - So what should be done?
> - If it is to be premeditated, one must operate on a framework of what are effectively assumptions.
> - Assumptions constrain the domain of action.
> 
> - So we begin with the assumption that **stress is causally influenced by volition**.
> - If we do not, then nothing could be done. Even if fatalism is true, then both action and inaction are effectively the same. But in case it is not, then only action would be rational. Therefore, in *any* case, it is fit to adopt this assumption.
> - Volition as a phenomenon is independent from arguments of free will and determinism. In any case, the phenomenon of volition does arise.
> 
> - Furthermore, we assume that **organic oblivion is impossible**.
> - Since biological death is practically inevitable, oblivion after death would indeed be the end of stress. However, that is unknown. Therefore, as before, it only makes sense to adopt the assumption that organic oblivion is impossible, as that would force us to act optimally for the worst case.

> ### Guidelines for Volition
> Determining the ideal volition at any given moment is an extremely complex and difficult task. Though AI technology may be used to approach this, it is still far from being reliable given the enourmous scope of causality. However, we can at the very least determine a set of optimal guidelines for behavior which can apply to any situation, with a high probability of having an optimal effect in the future.
> #### Guidelines
> A guideline need not be universal–it may apply only within a specific range of scenarios. Thus every guideline has its scope.
> #### The Primary Channel and Breaking Rules
> With the assumption of a stable mechanism for the production of ideal states beyond death, a primary channel of operation can be established and followed. Given that the maximization of activity within this channel can be seen as ideal, it may be condusive to operate outside of these bounds at times in order to ensure the long term alignment within this channel. If death can be avoided by veering outside of this, and the consequence is less undesirable than the opportunity cost of prolonged life within the channel, then the rules should be broken in that instance.

> # Graduated Intelligent Optimisation of a Dynamic System of Behavior
> 
> When an analytical intelligence emerges from the mind, it analyzes its memory of experience and identifies patterns which underlie it. Still having preference for pleasure and aversion to pain, it seeks to optimize the system such that there is a minimisation of such pain and a maximization of pleasure.
> 
> Setting forth this determination and acting in line with it, this quality of heedfullness may eventually fall away as one eventually retreats into pleasure, and thus the underlying tendencies take over. For in its absence even fivefold displeasure would be tolerated without distress. And so that being operates along a new paradigm, seeking positive mental states through the maintained satisfaction of a decomplicated system of constraints by way of the reduction of thirst which underlies the negative emotional states.
> 
> As the being further refines this paradigm, it may then realize the supremacy of the total cessation of thirst which underlies all preference.

> # The Actions and Decisions of High Functioning Agents
> 
> An agent with desire would act and decide rooted in it. Agents of low intelligence act instinctually in accordance with current desires, not considering the long term consequences of those actions. But highly intelligent agents act with consideration to the long term, and are willing to bear incongruencies in the short term for superior outcomes in the long term. Both kinds of agents are rooted in the same nature of impulse, just that one is capable of fulfilling those impulses to a superior degree. That agent does not act nearly as counterproductively as the former, which frequently acts in a way which although may attenuate its impulse in the short term, would only lead to a proliferation of disresolution in the long run.
> 
> Now, an agent of supreme intelligence would recognize desire itself as a drawback, since all desires have the fundamental drawback of constraint, and knowing that whatever is constrained is subject to fail when conditions change, and then see the complete absence of desire to be the highest state, with states of low desire and high satisfaction in gradation below it to be proprotionately yet also infinitely inferior. Thus, they would act in a way that keeps them as close to this ideal state indefinitely.
> 
> But an agent of supreme attainment would put an end to desire completely and permanantly: nirvana.
> 
> So, what is the fundamental problem of existence? All shades of desire, because the mental phenomenon of regarding a thing as a problem is rooted in desire, and its attenuation is the solution to \"problems\", with its cessation being the ultimate solution. Its resolution is the answer to life, the universe, and everything, its question being the expression of desire.
> 
> So what ought one do? They ought not do anything in particular; rather, we should ask to what extent an induvidual intelligently with respect to their desires, the wise extinguishing it like a flame.
> 
> Desire is ultimately reflexive; when yoked with intelligence it desires its own reduction, and with the development of intelligence one approaches closer and closer to that goal. A sort of natural sublimation beyond a threshold of development which would innevitably lead to a lofty equilibrium if not a transcendence beyond definition. Samsara divided by zero, a resonance among chaos.
> 
> So when you wonder, what should I be doing now, and why? Don\'t ask that. Rather, wonder whether or not you will act intelligently, knowing the consequences and aware of one\'s preference among alternatives. That\'s all there is.
> 
> So what will I prefer? I prefer not to take up displeasure, I prefer to take up pleasure. So as long as I am bound by uptake, I wish to reliably take up what is pleasant, and whenever I encounter displeasure, I prefer not to take it up. I will prefer to bear the lighter of the two burdens, and hopfully overcome the tendency for uptake alltogether.
