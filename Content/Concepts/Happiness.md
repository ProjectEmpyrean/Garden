> ## A Conservative and Liberal Definition of Happiness
> The conservative definition is quite well known: any state of experience satisfying the following conditions:
> 1. Emotional distress below a threshold
> 2. Pleasant feeling above a threshold
> 
> The liberal definition omits the second condition. Thus, bare happiness may exist with an experience of intense pain, but since there is little to no distress in response to it, it counts as happiness.
> 
> Is a conservative happiness superior to a liberal one even if the first factor is identical?

> ## Suppression and Cessation
> 
> There are two forms to the reduction of discontentment. The first and most conventional is that of satisfaction. When one gets what they want, their wishes fulfiled, craving satiated, that person is said to be happy and content. Posessing whatever is sought after, accompanied by pleasant feelings, their craving is temporarily abated. Whatever forms of pleasure and enjoyment exist, this is the satisfaction of craving which results in the reduction of discontentment -- mundane happiness. The second is in the form of relinquishment of that craving itself, direct elimination of the root cause of discontentment.
> 
> All solutions to this fundemental problem operate on these two mechanics. Profound contentment can be attained by having much and wanting little. But with the complete cessation of craving all together, stainless and without a trace left over, there comes an even greater possibility of complete liberation from all strife and objection, supreme happiness that is permanant and measureless.

> ## Beyond the Ideal
> 
> - The total cessation of all craving -- permanant happiness.
> 
> The essence of one potential ideal is attained through the total fulfilment of either of the factors governing discontentment. Getting exactly and whatever one wants immediately, there is the total fulfilment of satisfaction. And yet want nothing at all indefinitely, there is the total fulfilment by relinquishment. With the knowledge of the attainment and maintainence of this ideal state, acting in accordance whenever there is an opportunity for volition, one exists with supreme happiness in perpetuity.
