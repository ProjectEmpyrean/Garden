> ### Emotions
> With mental formations, feeling can be produced in the form of emotion. Emotions of negative valence produce mental displeasure, while positive ones produce pleasure. Displeasure tends to beget negative emotions, and pleasure tends to beget positive emotions.
> 
> Emotions form the range of manifestation of all stress. Whatever unhappiness there may be, it is an emotion.
> ### Equanimity
> It is possible to experience pleasure and displeasure without the generation of corresponding emotional states. See the simile of the arrow. Though difficult to conceive, displeasure is not inherently problematic, and is only made so when there is a proliferation of mental displeasure in the form of negative emotions.

> # Emotions and Their End
> 
> Negative emotions are characterized by acute thirst and resultant mental displeasure. Positive emotions are characterized by mild thirst, typically accompanied by mental pleasure. But completely devoid of thirst, no emotion would arise. Positive emotions are when the mind has what it wants, and relishes that experience. But the end of relishing would be even better, simply due to the absence of thirst.

> # The Origin of Emotions
> 
> Externally, one may feel 5 kinds of threefold sensory feeling. When one feels external displeasure with the underlying tendency of aversion towards displeasure, they also feel a mental displeasure in the form of emotion. Likewise when a person feels pleasure with the underlying tendancy of desrire for pleasure, they also feel a mental pleasure in the form of emotion. Thus we have positive and negative emotions. Now, a person may also feel positive emotions while experiencing external displeasure, and vice versa, and with permutations for netural feelings too. Thus, we have 18 different kinds of feeling.

> # Emotion as a Transformation of Valence
> 
> Emotion is modeled as an aggregate transformation of discrete valence components, visualized as two coressponding 2d mosaics with each cell a gradient from red to white to green, corresponding to its valence, and its size corresponding to its degree of cognizence. The bottom mosaic represents the feelings that are initially cognized, while the upper represents what is taken up, acquired, and transformed via egoic aquisition into emotions similarly measured in valence. We will see that in an ordinary being both planes would look rather similar, but to an arahant the top plane would invariantly remain blank.
